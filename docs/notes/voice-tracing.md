# Trace a spoken request

Run `./jarvis stats --voice --since 24h` to inspect `.jarvis/metrics.jsonl`. The existing usage and Barge-in reports retain their meanings. The voice view joins the capture pipeline's `turn`, the Hub's `user` turn and `speech` or `speech_stop` rows. It also shows capture backlog, reference submission and recognition evidence without printing transcripts.

## Correlation and clocks

`run_id` identifies one metrics-writer lifetime. Within that run, `request_at` is the exact speech-end timestamp carried unchanged by `Heard.at`, `SendTurn.at`, `Line.arrived` and `Speech.begin(heard_at=...)`. The pair is a request identifier. `turn` identifies the Hub agent turn and now appears on both its result and speech rows. Typed turns also have a request timestamp; Announcements have no captured request. Do not count either as physical spoken-request measurements. Old logs have no run identifier or complete correlation fields; the view labels them `legacy` and leaves missing fields unavailable.

All `*_at` fields described here use `time.perf_counter()` seconds in the running process. Tests inject a shared synthetic clock instead. The log's ISO `at` is the wall-clock write time, used for date filtering, not elapsed-time arithmetic. Capture and inference can finish long after the samples they process. Never order capture by the time its metrics row was written, join different runs on monotonic timestamps, or subtract component medians.

- A `turn` records `capture_start_at`, `request_at`, `endpoint_at`, its endpoint reason, Smart Turn assessments with capture and processing timestamps, and STT start/completion. The capture start includes retained pre-roll; it is not a labeled acoustic onset. `end_wait_ms`, `speech_s`, `smart_turn_ms` and `stt_ms` keep their existing definitions.
- A Hub `user` row records `turn`, `request_at`, `started_at` and `completed_at`. Its existing `wait_ms` includes endpointing, transcription, delivery and waiting for the Talker. It is not an isolated agent-queue measurement.
- `speech.first_meaningful_audio_ms` runs from request end to the first generated sentence's estimated playback start. Cached speech and chimes do not satisfy it. `first_audio_ms` retains its old meaning, which can include cached speech in legacy logs. Timer-injected holding speech and its `holding_line` metric are removed; older logs may still contain them. `meaningful_audio_basis=queue_estimate` makes the limitation explicit: device buffering and physical speaker output are not measured. No generated audio gives `null`, not zero.
- `speech_stop` records the affected Hub `turn`, `request_at`, stop time and any first generated-audio estimate, including when generation never reaches its normal completion row. Only replies still generating, synthesizing or owning queued playback get a stop row; later chimes do not keep a completed reply alive. A stop request does not establish that the device has finished its fade or drained its buffer.
- `voice` transitions record event time separately from processing time. `recognition` records candidate onset (`candidate_at`), captured-through time and STT processing times. It is transcription evidence, not a semantic-admission verdict. A Barge-in's existing decision row carries the same unrounded `candidate_at`.
- `capture` summaries report captured-through time, processing start/completion, maximum queue delay in the interval, frame count, queued frames and dropped frames. They are emitted every 100 processed frames, or at catch-up after a material delay or drops. These are interval summaries, not per-request attributions. Audio dropped at shutdown before another summary may not be reported.
- `playback_reference` reports a callback block number, submission timestamp, its actual sample count/rate and cumulative submitted samples. It observes the blocks handed to echo cancellation, including silence, rather than the original synthesized clip. Ordinary logging samples these rows at most once per second; skipped block numbers expose that sampling. With opt-in audio clips enabled, each reference block and processed capture frame gets timing metadata for targeted alignment work.

`device_captured_at` and `device_played_at` are explicitly unavailable (`null`). The current microphone stamp is the callback's monotonic time, not the PortAudio ADC timestamp; the reference timestamp is callback submission, not the DAC timestamp. These observations can expose queue backlog and disconnected reference flow, but cannot establish acoustic alignment, self-echo, speaker attribution or physical latency. Use targeted device timing evidence for those claims before replacing an engine.

## Reproducible synthetic evidence

The tests use the existing Hub, capture and player seams, synthetic marker frames and fake engines. They open no microphone, speakers, subscription session or GitHub mutation.

- `tests/test_input_pipeline.py::test_trace_distinguishes_premature_endpoint_from_a_later_continuation` feeds 0.8 seconds of speech, 0.6 seconds of silence, then 0.7 seconds of resumed speech. The current endpoint policy commits twice. The two request identifiers, overlapping pre-roll and delayed processing timestamp make the split visible. This is a regression fixture for later policy work, not proof that the User's saved pause cases have this cause.
- `tests/test_capture.py::test_trace_preserves_capture_and_playback_times_through_an_inference_stall` holds a wake inference while timestamped input waits and an actual `SoundPlayer` callback supplies a 1024-sample reference block at 24 kHz. After release, the capture summary reports a 990 ms maximum queue delay and a later processing time while the reference retains its earlier submission time. Playback buffering is unchanged.
- `tests/test_speech.py::test_nonverbal_cues_do_not_claim_a_meaningful_response` plays a nonverbal readiness cue at time zero and generated content 800 ms later. Only the latter supplies meaningful latency. The Hub interruption test requires a stopped-turn trace even when the generation result arrives after the stop.
- `tests/test_stats.py::test_voice_trace_explains_slow_and_superseded_requests_without_cached_latency` exercises the diagnostic command on reviewed synthetic rows: 500 ms endpoint wait, 120 ms STT, 700 ms total Hub wait and 2400 ms estimated meaningful response. A cached first-audio value of 1500 ms is excluded. A superseded request with no generated audio stays unavailable; capture drops and candidate recognition remain visible separately.

These fixtures do not establish that live echo is present, that AEC is disconnected, or why a particular personal recording fragmented. They make those hypotheses distinguishable without changing endpoint, interruption, playback or tool policy.

## Local evidence and retention

Timing metadata contains no new transcript or raw audio fields. Detailed timing follows the existing `JARVIS_AUDIO_CLIPS=on` opt-in; clips and sidecars continue to use the configured count, age and size retention. No new raw or reference audio recorder is introduced. Existing transcripts and opted-in clips remain under gitignored `.jarvis/` and may be personal. Keep them local. Publish only reviewed synthetic cases and aggregate results. Missing device measurements, failed samples and requests with no meaningful audio must remain visible when reporting acceptance results.

## Request-specific replies

Normal replies stream the generated answer without a timer-injected holding sentence. Slow generation stays silent until that content arrives. The Talker prompt requires useful content first for direct answers and a short request-specific response before background work tools. Prompt and tool descriptions present internal coordination as Jarvis's own work. Startup and unavailable cached speech are removed. No conversational sentence is cached; readiness and Wake word cues remain nonverbal.

The scripted EN/IT speech tests check that generated text can play before delegation starts and that cancelling slow generation before its first text emits no filler. They establish pipeline ordering and preserved language/voice selection, not semantic relevance from a real model. In an authorized real evaluation, assess whether the opening addresses the request, preserves the established character, avoids internal handoff narration and asks required Approval questions exactly.

## Readiness and unavailable status

Startup and failure messages use the existing terminal notice channel, with no new layout or color. `Model loading` precedes the hidden warm-up; `Model ready` follows a successful turn and is withheld while a Usage limit remains active. `Speech ready` follows voice loading and queues a nonverbal readiness tone. `Listening ready` follows engine loading and an open microphone source. These are separate component states, not a physical acceptance measurement. No timer inserts thinking notices.

Usage-limit notices include the known reset time or `Reset time unavailable`, plus the next step. They do not claim the voice floor, synthesize a fallback or consume a model turn. A spoken request made while held still finishes its silent reply lifecycle, so listening can resume. A failed backend reports the actual error and a retry or restart step. Typed input and `--text` keep working through the existing lifecycle; a startup authentication failure still needs a restart.

The notice is considered delivered when shown, including a limit found during warm-up or while another reply plays. A restart shows an active saved limit once so the new terminal explains why work waits. Explicit requests made while held show its current status again. Role holds, queued work, retry backoff and persisted reset evidence keep the existing Usage limit policy. Clearing a timer produces no recovery claim. Only a successful turn of a held role confirms recovery; its on-screen notice appears once and the existing generated Announcement still follows Quiet mode and quiet-time scheduling. Real-model relevance and physical playback remain opt-in.

## Spoken request revisions

`request_id` and `revision` now join Hub `user`, `speech` and `speech_stop` rows across a resumed thought. `request_at` still measures the latest speech end; it is no longer the sole ownership key. `spoken_relation` rows record request/revision, candidate identity, verdict and reason without adding candidate text to the metric. Candidate context and transcripts remain local.

Input `turn` rows include `capture_epoch`, `candidate` and `speech_started_at` separately from pre-roll `capture_start_at`; Orchestrator `task` rows include the Task producer revisions. Capture epochs retire late input after Wake, typed replacement or shutdown. Candidate sequences prevent duplicate admission within an epoch. A speech onset freezes the logical phase used for assessment; queued reply estimates do not claim physical hearing. Locally admitted fragments retain one generation identity and increment revision. A local routing label cannot discard history or choose Task scope; the admitted Talker resolves the affected Tasks and any new raw semantic boundary. Stale decisions and reply callbacks cannot take the current revision's floor. ADR 0016 describes retention and readiness bounds; fake-adapter coverage establishes the mechanism independently of model quality.


## Silent end controls and generated farewells

A recognized whole-utterance end control in a capturing state returns idle without speech or another model turn. Matching remains whole-utterance: longer requests containing thanks or control words still reach the Talker. Typed input keeps its generated-text route. The Talker's `end_conversation` tool still lets an actual generated reply finish before idle, without a Follow-up window.

The stop-control path adds no new playback fast path and does not admit transcripts outside the current capturing states. Its Hub regressions cover EN/IT endings, longer requests, generated farewell playback, typed controls and silent idling. They do not establish real-model contextual admission or acoustic recognition accuracy; background speech in an open Follow-up window remains subject to the separate semantic-admission work.

Pending spoken scopes and typed clarification are durable Hub state. Scope IDs, generation revisions and resolver attempts fence stale choices. Selected Task producer revisions commit atomically before work; unresolved/expired holds do not release old Approval authority. Restart sends pending, already-admitted context to a text-only Talker turn before held Task recovery. No failed local recognizer is wired; semantic/privacy, real worker and physical acceptance gates remain separate.
