# A restart keeps Tasks and their waits, and tells the Orchestrator at once

Story 53 asks that a Hub restart lose no follow-ups. The Hub saves its open Tasks, their Subagents, Watchers and Checks, and the Orchestrator's messages not yet answered to `.jarvis/state.json` each time they change: one small JSON file, versioned, written to a temporary file and renamed over the old one. A file that is not valid JSON or has another version is moved to `state.unreadable.json` with a notice, and the Hub starts with nothing restored rather than failing. A file with any record it cannot read is set aside the same way, before anything restored from it starts. Times are saved as wall-clock times, converted from the Hub's monotonic clock, so a Check keeps its due time across the restart: one that came due while the Hub was down fires as soon as it is back, once, and its next repeat waits from then. A wall clock set back meanwhile never makes a Check wait longer than its interval or a Watcher longer than its limit. Finished Tasks are not saved, and neither is the Talker's conversation: each launch starts a fresh Talker (story 24).

On restart, a Watcher runs its `gh` command again, checked anew against the Org, within what is left of its six-hour limit. A Subagent the old Hub never saw end is marked `interrupted`. Its branch and worktree are kept, and its worktree lock, held under its id, is released the way its own end would have released it, with the branch described for the Orchestrator; a reading Subagent's worktree is removed unless it holds something. The Hub releases a lock only while it still names that Subagent: the User may have taken a stale one over, and the Orchestrator then hears who holds it. The lock's holder is gone with the old process, and a lock left behind would keep the sdlc `sweep` off that worktree for good. A clean close does the same for the Subagents it stops, so the next launch only reports them.

## The recovery message goes at once, and only when something was pending

When the saved state holds an open Task, the Hub sends the Orchestrator one `<event source="hub" reason="restart">` message in the background as soon as it starts, listing those Tasks, their restored waits, interrupted Subagents and the unanswered messages, each in full: a Subagent's final report, a Watcher's end or a Check's last firing that sat in the queue reaches the Orchestrator this way. The restored Watchers and Checks start only once it is queued, so an overdue Check comes after it. What to do with it is in the Orchestrator's prompt, not the message. That turn covers every listed Task, so its tools take a `task_id`. A Task nothing will wake (an interrupted Subagent, or a turn the old Hub cut off) that the turn leaves without a report fails, and the User hears so. With nothing pending, no message goes and no model call follows the Talker's warm-up.

## Considered Options

- Hold the message until the Orchestrator is first needed. Rejected: a Task whose Subagent was interrupted has nothing left to wake the Orchestrator, so "first needed" could be never, and holding it for the User's first Task would put a second Task's work in front of theirs.
- Restart interrupted Subagents on their own. Rejected: a Subagent half way through may have pushed or opened a PR, so whether to start over is a judgment for the Orchestrator with the branch in front of it.

## Consequences

- A launch after a restart with open Tasks spends one Orchestrator turn of the Max limits. Story 1 still holds as ADR 0006 reads it: the Talker is ready at once and the message runs in the background.
- A message stays saved until the Orchestrator's turn on it ends, so one whose turn the restart cut off comes again in the recovery message, and work that turn had started may show in the listing beside it.
- The Usage limit is saved too, each stopped role with its reset time and how often the limit has hit it since its last successful turn, so a restart neither calls a limited session nor tells the User again; one that reset while the Hub was down clears at launch, and its one Announcement comes when a held session next completes a turn. News the limited Talker could not tell is saved and told after the reset. A Subagent still queued (behind the cap or the limit) never started, so unlike an interrupted one it is kept queued with its worktree and lock, and starts on its own; the Hub saves the moment one starts, so a crash never leaves a running Subagent saved as queued.
- `close()` stops each wait without reporting it, so the state file keeps it waiting; the notice that Checks were dropped on close is gone.
