# Revise spoken requests before replacing their generation

A spoken request keeps its identity when the User resumes or corrects the same thought after its first transcript reached the Talker. Its revision changes. Opening and additional speech remain verbatim and in order; the local assessor does not rewrite the User's intent. A clearly new request receives a new identity. The Hub still owns Tasks, conversational admission and tool authority.

The input pipeline stamps each captured turn with its capture epoch and candidate sequence. An utterance begun while the request is thinking survives the start of reply playback. Wake, typed replacement and shutdown retire the epoch. Reply callbacks and Talker inputs carry the request identity and revision, so an old reply cannot finish the replacement's voice turn. Ownership changes before interruption is awaited; obsolete Talker tools are refused while the old generation drains.

A bounded local assessment receives the candidate transcript, current request, current available played text at assessment start and the logical phase frozen at captured onset. The played text is a queue/progress estimate, not an onset snapshot. A thinking-origin candidate can therefore carry nonempty played text when reply audio began before its delayed assessment. started_before_reply is true or false when owner/onset history establishes that relation, and null when unavailable. Continuation and correction both revise the current owner; a new request replaces it. An explicit stop_reply verdict stops only the owned reply and reopens bounded silent listening, preserving background Task identities. Unrelated, uncertain and unavailable results preserve the existing generation and audio and cannot reach the Talker. The Follow-up window continues to admit a subsequent user turn normally. Logical reply startup and queued played text do not establish precisely what a physical listener heard.

Confirmed unrelated fragments are discarded. Uncertain fragments are reassessed in order together with later observed speech; the combined assessment retains the earliest onset context and its full transcript is the one admitted. A newer known onset or transcript prevents an in-flight partial decision from being admitted on its own. No speech that has not yet arrived is inferred. Candidates are retained within the owned exchange: at most four waiting or unresolved fragments, a 6,000-character combined request, and the pipeline's existing maximum turn duration. Reaching a limit reports that the User must repeat or split the request; a duration-limited fragment is not silently admitted as a completed request. Wake, typed replacement, idle and shutdown release retained context. A revision reuses its single previously delegated Task. With several prior Tasks, the Talker must identify an owned task_id or explicitly request additional work with new_task=true. Conflicting or unowned Task references are refused.

At revision admission, owned Tasks keep their identity, retain the accepted request text and increment their producer revision. The synchronous intent and cancellation mutations save as one batch before awaiting interruption. The Hub withdraws their old pending Approvals, cancels old waits and Subagents, and removes obsolete queued news. Orchestrator inputs, Approvals, Subagents, Watchers and Checks carry the Task revision durably. Restore withdraws or retires old producers before recovery; obsolete prepared worktrees are cleaned without opening an agent session. Active tools and delayed producers are checked against the Task revision; async Approval reads and Subagent preparation are checked again after they return. An already-running irreversible action cannot be undone by this mechanism. Exact Approval questions and target checks retain ADR 0013's authority.

A local worker may load in the background under a separate bounded readiness deadline. While warming or after readiness fails, assessment returns uncertain. Candidate deadlines apply to the ready worker; a timed-out worker is terminated. A background-prepared worker does not repeatedly cold-load on each candidate after such a timeout.

This decision supplies the ownership mechanism for #38 and the endpoint follow-up in #34. It does not select a recognition model or establish the interruption semantics in #39/#40. Fake-adapter tests establish ownership and cancellation behavior. Selecting a production assessor still requires independent accuracy, real-worker readiness and coexistence evidence; failed research candidates remain disconnected. ADRs 0014 and 0015 retain their behavior and latency requirements.

The mechanical checkpoint is partial acceptance for #38. No production local recognizer is selected or wired; injected fake decisions establish the ownership behavior, and the default thinking-phase path remains uncertain. Independent semantic accuracy, actual worker deadline/readiness and engine coexistence gates remain pending.
