# The iPhone ducks music under Jarvis and levels speech to -16 LUFS

On the iPhone, music stopped whenever Jarvis spoke, and Jarvis's voice was much quieter than the music. Replies measured about -25 LUFS and music about -14 to -16 LUFS. The User turned the volume up to hear Jarvis, and the music came back too loud when it resumed. On 2026-11-27 the User settled three things: music keeps playing while Jarvis speaks and while Live mode is on, ducked instead of paused; with AirPods, output stays in full-quality A2DP and the iPhone's microphone records instead of the hands-free call profile; and speech plays at -16 LUFS.

Every audio session the app opens mixes with other audio and ducks it. A reply outside Live mode and the paused-Talk preview use `playback` with `spokenAudio` and the options `duckOthers` and `interruptSpokenAudioAndMixWithOthers`: music ducks, and a podcast or audiobook pauses because two voices at once are unintelligible. A Talk recording and Live mode use `playAndRecord` with `duckOthers`, `defaultToSpeaker` and `allowBluetoothA2DP`, without `allowBluetoothHFP`. With A2DP alone, iOS keeps AirPods as a stereo output and records from the iPhone's built-in microphone. Releasing a session deactivates it with `notifyOthersOnDeactivation`, so ducked music returns to its own level and a paused podcast can resume. Live mode keeps its session active for as long as it is on, so music stays ducked for that time. Voice processing ducks other audio far harder by default, so Live mode sets its ducking configuration to advanced ducking at the minimum level and leaves the session's duck as the level music plays at.

Speech is levelled on the iPhone, not in the Hub. The Hub keeps one speech reference level, `SPEECH_LUFS` (-25 LUFS), which is Kokoro's English level and the level the Mac's speech gate, Barge-in thresholds and echo measurements were tuned on (ADR 0029). Each cloned voice has a fixed trim to that level. Measured with ffmpeg's `ebur128` over five sentences per language, English needed the existing -2 dB and Italian -4.3 dB, because the Italian reference clip is 2.5 LU louder. The chime sounds at `SPEECH_LUFS` while it plays and the closing sound 2 LU below it, instead of 6 LU and 3 LU above the voice. On the iPhone, every speech output, Live mode's included, plays through a peak limiter with +11 dB of pre-gain and then a -1.5 dB cut at the main mixer. Rendered offline from Hub sentences, that measures -15.6 LUFS in English and -16.5 LUFS in Italian, with a true peak of -1.4 dBTP. The gains are `SpeechLoudness` constants in `SpeechPlayer.swift`.

## Considered Options

- Pause music while Jarvis speaks or while Live mode is on. Rejected by the User: music should keep playing.
- Keep the hands-free profile so AirPods record. Rejected by the User: HFP drops music and speech to call quality for as long as the session is active. With the phone in a pocket or across the room, its microphone is farther from the User than the AirPods' would be; device checks will show what that costs the Wake word. iOS 26 offers `bluetoothHighQualityRecording` for AirPods recording without HFP; it was not tried.
- Raise the voice in the Hub for every Client. Rejected: the Mac's gate, Barge-in thresholds and echo measurements were tuned at the current level, and louder Mac playback would put more echo into its microphone.
- Mix without ducking in Live mode and let voice processing's advanced ducking lower music only while someone speaks. Not chosen: the User asked for music ducked while Live mode is on, and music at full level on the speaker would reach the microphone the Wake word listens on. It remains the alternative if the constant duck proves too deep.

## Consequences

- On the Mac, Italian replies are 2.3 dB quieter than before, now level with English. The chime is 6 dB quieter and the closing sound about 5 dB quieter. Mac echo can only fall.
- How far iOS ducks music, how much voice processing attenuates the reply in Live mode, the AirPods route, and whether a reply at -16 LUFS from the phone speaker still leaves the Barge-in judge and the Wake word working are device checks, listed in `ios/README.md`.
