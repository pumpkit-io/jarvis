# Assess cumulative speech separately from taking the floor

Jarvis assesses accumulating speech and pause history repeatedly, before the old acoustic endpoint, to distinguish directed speech, taking the User floor and Reply readiness. A directed Interruption stops output as soon as the floor decision is established; it does not itself start another reply or cancel a Task. When completion is ambiguous, preserving the User's thought takes priority over the earliest possible reply. Clearly completed turns retain the existing meaningful-response latency targets.

The User accepted Jev as the preferred provider on 2026-11-06, based on successful use in Pumpkit and existing credits. They also accepted sending bounded candidate transcripts, including potentially unrelated speech, pause timings and recent conversation context to Jev. Raw audio stays local. This revises ADR 0014's local-first/cloud-decision restriction for this explicit Jev integration; its directedness requirement, protection of Backchannels and separation of idle activation remain. Provider speed in Pumpkit is useful experience, not Jarvis acceptance evidence. The implementation must qualify the voice decisions rather than replace one endpoint classifier with another.

Speech that began before reply startup survives that startup. New speech during a reply starts a fresh talking period with the preceding request available as bounded context. Confirmed unrelated words do not enter conversational input; uncertain words remain briefly available for reassessment. Partial transcript revisions replace their earlier hypotheses rather than duplicating them. Source ownership and accepted raw words remain governed by ADR 0016.

If classification fails or directedness is unknown, playback continues and the failure is visible. An immediate local keyboard stop remains available independently of Jev, and typed interaction continues to work. Missed directed voice stops count as failures. This preserves background-speech immunity without mistaking universal abstention for success.

The accepted pause evaluation includes 0.5, 1, 2 and 3 second gaps, both mid-thought and after complete-sounding sentences, alongside clearly finished controls. These are cases to assess, not a universal waiting period. Cadence, stability, confidence thresholds, resource limits and numerical interruption-delay gates remain evidence-led implementation work. Live evaluation needs a bounded call/cost plan and agreed device conditions; this planning decision does not start provider calls, spend credits, open audio devices or authorize a new purchase.
